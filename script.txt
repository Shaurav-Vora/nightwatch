NIGHTWATCH - DEMO SCRIPT, ABOUT 2 MIN 55
=================================

Read the NARRATION lines aloud. Do what the ACTION lines say while you read
them. Pace is about 150 words a minute, which is unhurried. That puts the
speech at roughly 2:20, leaving room for pauses inside a 3 minute limit.


BEFORE YOU RECORD
-----------------
[ ] Open https://shaurav-vora.github.io/nightwatch/ in a clean window. No
    bookmarks bar, no extensions showing.
[ ] Browser zoom 100%. Window at least 1600 wide, or the three columns of the
    app will stack.
[ ] Load both pages once, then reload. Cold tiles look like lag on camera.
[ ] On the map page, click Skip on the tour. You are the tour.
[ ] Leave it on Phoenix, layer "Hottest every time", filter at 100%, and the
    unit switch in the city bar on °F. Every spoken figure assumes °F.
[ ] Record at 1080p or better. Move the mouse slower than feels natural.


0:00 - 0:20   WHAT IT IS
-------------
ACTION   Landing page, top. Hold on the title and the opening line.

NARRATION
  Cities decide where to plant trees and where to open cooling centres using
  maps of how hot it gets. NIGHTWATCH maps something else: how many hours a
  day each city block spends above its danger threshold. Phoenix, Houston and
  Chicago.


0:20 - 0:45   THE TWO MAPS
-------------
ACTION   Scroll to the pair of Phoenix maps. Let both sit on screen while you
         talk. Point at the left one, then the right one.

NARRATION
  Here is the same afternoon in Phoenix, asked two ways. On the left, how hot
  it got. On the right, how long it stayed dangerous. Take the hundred hottest
  blocks and the hundred that stayed dangerous longest. Not one block is on both
  lists, so a city planning from the left map misses all hundred.


0:45 - 1:22   THE TOGGLE
-------------
ACTION   Click "Open the map". Wait for Phoenix to draw.
         Click "Hours dangerously hot" first, so both views cover the same
         single day. Then click "Afternoon heat" and hold two seconds.
         Then click back to "Hours dangerously hot".

NARRATION
  [screen: Phoenix, "Hours dangerously hot"]
  This is the app. Phoenix, one day in July. Each square is a hundred metres
  across, about one city block. The colour is how many hours that block spent
  above ninety-five Fahrenheit. Red is longest.

  [click "Afternoon heat". Say the next line as it appears, then hold three
   seconds so the viewer can look at it]
  Now the same blocks, same afternoon, as temperature. The map a city uses
  today. It looks dramatic, but read the scale: the whole city fits inside three
  degrees.

  [click back to "Hours dangerously hot"]
  And back to hours. On temperature these blocks sit within three degrees of
  each other. On hours they run from six to fourteen. Same blocks, same
  afternoon. The hours map tells you which ones to fix first. The temperature
  map cannot.


1:22 - 1:42   TWO REAL BLOCKS
-------------
ACTION   Scroll the right panel to "Two blocks, same afternoon". Hold long
         enough for the coordinates to be readable.

NARRATION
  The app searches Phoenix for two blocks that read the same temperature at
  three in the afternoon. These two both read ninety-nine point seven
  Fahrenheit, so a temperature map cannot tell them apart. One spends six hours
  above the danger threshold, the other eleven. Five hours, at the same
  temperature.


1:42 - 2:14   THE SHORTLIST, AND WHO IS IN IT
-------------
ACTION   Scroll to "Hottest areas, for longest". Click the top row and let the
         map fly and outline it. Then scroll on to "Who lives there".
         Stay on Phoenix for this beat.

NARRATION
  This is the output. Joined-up patches of blocks that stayed worst on every
  hot day, ordered by residents rather than by size. The top one holds three
  thousand four hundred people and is only the fourth largest. The largest holds
  seven hundred and ninety-seven. Rank by size and you start in the wrong
  place.


2:14 - 2:42   DOES IT REPEAT
-------------
ACTION   Click "Houston" in the bar above the map, then click "Hottest every
         time" so the map covers all five dates and the stamp lists them.
         Scroll the right panel to "Does it repeat?", showing 1,408 and 64.6x.

NARRATION
  The obvious objection is one bad afternoon, so we tested five hot days
  between twenty twenty-two and twenty twenty-five.
  In Houston, fourteen hundred blocks landed in the hottest quarter of the city
  every single time. Chance would give you twenty-two. That is sixty-five times
  over. Not weather. The built environment, and a city can change it.


2:42 - 3:00   WHAT YOU CAN DO WITH IT
-------------
ACTION   Scroll the right panel to the download buttons. Click "Generate full
         report". Let it open and scroll it once. End on the report or the map.

NARRATION
  All of it exports. Ranked sites as a spreadsheet, the grid as GeoJSON, and a
  printable report carrying the method and the limitations. Built on
  FortyGuard's API, three cities, fifty-three thousand blocks. Heat maps measure
  how hot. This measures how long.


IF YOU RUN LONG
---------------
Cut in this order:
  1. "Five hours, at the same temperature." at 1:22. The card on screen

  2. Start 0:00 at "NIGHTWATCH maps something else".
  3. "Rank by size and you start in the wrong place." at 1:42.

Do not cut the toggle at 0:45. That toggle is the argument, and the video does
