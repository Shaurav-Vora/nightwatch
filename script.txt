NIGHTWATCH - 3 MINUTE DEMO SCRIPT
=================================

Read the NARRATION lines aloud. Do what the ACTION lines say while you read
them. Target pace is about 150 words per minute, which is unhurried. If you
run long, the cut list is at the bottom.


BEFORE YOU RECORD
-----------------
[ ] Open https://shaurav-vora.github.io/nightwatch/ in a clean window, no
    bookmarks bar, no extensions visible.
[ ] Browser zoom 100%. Window at least 1600 wide so the three columns fit.
[ ] Hard-refresh once (Ctrl+Shift+R) and let both pages load fully, then
    reload to start. Cold tile loading looks like lag on camera.
[ ] On the map page, click "Skip" on the tour. You are the tour.
[ ] Reset to Phoenix, layer "Hottest every time", filter slider at 100%.
[ ] Record at 1080p minimum. Mouse movements slow and deliberate.


0:00 - 0:22   THE PROBLEM
-------------
ACTION   Landing page, top. Slowly scroll to the two cards.

NARRATION
  Cities decide where to plant trees and open cooling centres using maps of how
  hot it gets. We measured every hundred-metre block across eighty-three square
  miles of Phoenix on a hot July afternoon. From the coolest block to the
  hottest, the whole spread was one point eight degrees. Nobody acts on that.

ACTION   Stop with both cards on screen.


0:22 - 0:42   THE REFRAME
-------------
ACTION   Point at the "9.1 hours" card. Then scroll to the quote block.

NARRATION
  So we asked a different question. Not how hot, but how many hours in a row do
  you stay above the danger threshold. That answer spans nine hours. Same
  blocks, same day. One of those numbers is a rounding error. The other is a
  public health problem.


0:42 - 1:08   THE MAP, AND THE TOGGLE
-------------
ACTION   Click "Open the map". Wait for Phoenix to render.
         Then click "Afternoon heat". Pause two seconds on it.
         Then click back to "Hottest every time".

NARRATION
  This is Phoenix. Red is the ground that stays dangerous longest after dark.
  Here is the map a city would use today.
  [PAUSE while Afternoon heat is on screen]
  Temperature at three in the afternoon. Almost flat. Now back to duration.
  [CLICK BACK]
  Completely different city. In Phoenix, how hot a block gets in the afternoon
  tells you almost nothing about its night. R-squared is zero point one.


1:08 - 1:30   TWO REAL BLOCKS
-------------
ACTION   Scroll the right panel to the twin-blocks card. Let the coordinates
         be readable for at least three seconds.

NARRATION
  What that means on the ground. These two blocks both read ninety-nine point
  seven Fahrenheit at three p.m. Identical on a temperature map. One spends six
  hours above the danger threshold. The other spends eleven. Real coordinates.


1:30 - 1:55   DOES IT REPEAT
-------------
ACTION   Click "Houston" in the bar above the map. Scroll the right panel to
         the repeat card showing 1,408 and 64.6x.

NARRATION
  The obvious objection is that this was one bad afternoon, so we tested it
  first, across five heat events between twenty twenty-two and twenty
  twenty-five. In Houston, fourteen hundred blocks landed in the worst quarter
  of the city every single time. Chance would give you twenty-two. That is
  sixty-five times over. Not weather. Built environment, which means fixable.


1:55 - 2:18   WHO LIVES THERE
-------------
ACTION   Scroll to the population card, then to the ranked list. Click the
         top row. Let the map fly and outline the site.

NARRATION
  Then we joined census population. Twenty-nine thousand people live in that
  core, but the density there is below the city average, because the hottest
  ground is largely industrial. So the list is ordered by people, not area, and
  it shows you which sites move when you do that.


2:18 - 2:40   THE PART THAT FAILED
-------------
ACTION   Switch to the landing page tab, scroll to "What we got wrong".

NARRATION
  One more thing. The idea we started with was that the hottest block at three
  p.m. is the safest at three a.m. Chicago agreed strongly. It was Lake
  Michigan. Exclude land within three kilometres of water and it flips. We
  abandoned it. That failure is still in the repo, because a result nobody
  tried to break is not worth much.


2:40 - 3:00   WHAT YOU CAN DO WITH IT
-------------
ACTION   Back to the map tab. Click "Generate full report". Let the report
         page open and scroll it once. End on the report or the map.

NARRATION
  Everything exports. Ranked sites as a spreadsheet, the grid as GeoJSON, and a
  printable report with the method and the limitations in it. Built on
  FortyGuard's API. Three cities, fifty-three thousand blocks. Heat maps measure
  how hot. This measures how long.


IF YOU RUN LONG
---------------
Cut in this order:
  1. The last two sentences of 1:08 (the twin-blocks coordinates line).
  2. The whole 2:18 failed-hypothesis beat. Costs you the honesty point, so
     cut it last if you can.
  3. Tighten 0:00 by starting at "We measured every hundred-metre block".

DO NOT CUT the Afternoon heat toggle at 0:42. That toggle is the entire
argument and the video is pointless without it.
